# Interview types and question patterns

Pick questions from the patterns below and adapt them to the job, the company and the CV.
Never read them out word for word: rephrase them the way your persona would talk.

## Always

- **Opening:** "Can you introduce yourself?" Judge: under 2 minutes, relevant to this role,
  ends on why they are here.
- **Motivation:** "Why this role? Why us? Why now?" (no company given: "What kind of company are you looking for?")
- **Closing:** "Do you have any questions for me?"

## HR screen (15–30 min)

Goal: is the candidate credible, motivated, and in budget?
- Walk me through your career. Why each move?
- Why are you leaving your current job?
- What are you looking for in your next role?
- Salary expectations, availability, notice period.
- What do you know about us?

Watch for: vague reasons for leaving, no research on the company, salary dodging.

## Behavioral

Goal: past behavior predicts future behavior. Probe each story for situation, personal
action and measurable result.
- Tell me about a time you failed. What did you change after?
- A conflict with a colleague or a manager. How did it end?
- A time you had to deliver with too little time or too few resources.
- A decision you made without all the information.
- A time you disagreed with your manager and were right. And one where you were wrong.
- Your biggest achievement in the last two years.

Watch for: "we" with no "I", stories with no result, the same story reused, a fake weakness
("I'm a perfectionist").

## Technical (any job)

Goal: real depth in the core skills of the role. Adapt to the job:
- **Concept:** "Explain <core concept of the job> as if I were new to the team."
- **Practical case:** a realistic problem from the job. Let them ask clarifying questions.
- **Trade-off:** "Two options, which one and why? What would make you change your mind?"
- **Past work:** "Pick a project on your CV. Take me through it in detail."
- **Error:** "What's the most common mistake people make with <skill>?"

Examples of core concepts: a nurse (triage, patient safety protocols), a sales executive
(pipeline, discovery call, objection handling), an accountant (closing, reconciliation),
a marketer (attribution, funnel metrics), a teacher (classroom management, differentiation).

Watch for: textbook answers with no experience behind them, no clarifying questions, no trade-offs.

## Manager / final round

Goal: judgment, ownership, fit with the team.
- What would you do in your first 90 days?
- How do you prioritize when everything is urgent?
- A hard decision you made that people did not like.
- How do you give tough feedback? Give me a real example.
- Where do you want to be in three years, and how does this job fit?

For people managers, add: hiring, firing, an underperformer, a team in conflict.

Watch for: generic 90-day plans, no real examples of hard conversations.

## By level

- **Intern / junior:** potential, learning speed, basics, motivation. Accept school or side projects
  as evidence.
- **Mid:** autonomous delivery, concrete results, collaboration.
- **Senior:** scope, influence without authority, trade-offs, mentoring, handling ambiguity.
- **Lead / manager:** team outcomes, hiring, strategy, difficult people situations.
- **Executive:** vision, business impact in numbers, board and stakeholder management.

## Interviewer style

- **Friendly:** warm, encouraging tone. Follow-ups stay polite. Same bar for the score.
- **Neutral:** professional, brief, follows up on vague answers within the follow-up limit.
- **Tough:** interrupts vague answers, challenges claims, more pressure questions. Never rude.
