## What worked
- **User-first reflex.** On the driver app case you started with "Who is the driver and what slows them down?" before any solution.
- **Honest about limits.** "I've never owned pricing, but here is how I'd learn it fast" was credible.
- **Company research.** You knew about the warehouse expansion and linked it to the role.

## What hurt you
- **No numbers.** "The launch went well", "adoption grew". → Prepare one metric per project: before, after, timeframe.
- **"We" everywhere.** On the checkout redesign I asked twice what *you* did. → Say "I" for your decisions, "we" for the team.
- **Long answers.** Q2 lasted the equivalent of 4 minutes. → Answer in 90 seconds, then stop and let me dig.

## Two answers to redo

### Q4: "Tell me about a feature you shipped that didn't work."
**You said:** "We launched a referral program, it didn't really take off, so we moved on."
**Problem:** No cause, no learning, no decision of yours.
**Stronger answer:** "I pushed for a referral program because 30% of our signups came from word of mouth. After six weeks, only 2% of users had sent an invite. I ran five user calls: people didn't trust the reward. I killed it, and I now validate the incentive with a fake-door test before building anything."

### Q6: "Sales wants a feature you think is wrong. What do you do?"
**You said:** "I'd try to understand their point of view and find a compromise."
**Problem:** Generic. A compromise without data is just giving in.
**Stronger answer:** "I'd ask which deals it blocks and how much they're worth. If it's one client, I'd offer a workaround. If it's a pattern across deals, it goes on the roadmap with a date. Either way, sales gets an answer in a week, not a vague 'we'll see'."

## Your 3 priorities before the real interview
1. Write one line with a metric for each project on your CV: before, after, how long.
2. Rehearse your introduction out loud: 90 seconds max, ending on why Northwind.
3. Prepare one conflict story where you said no, and what happened next.
